The two names are one filing apart

The original post asserts that Naomi Osagiede also files as Naomi Egah. That is not inference, and it does not require comparing signatures or addresses across documents. It is on a single record.

The last line matters separately: an entity in this web is itself listed as the manager of another entity in this web. The same nesting appears at Beyond Healthcare Agency LLC, whose registered managers are “Naomi A Osagiede” and an entity called Beyond Trust — which exists in the register as Beyond Trust Int LLC, filed under Egah.

Searching only the Osagiede surname returns 20 entities. Searching Egah returns 26. Two overlap. Anyone who searched one name and stopped saw less than half the structure — which is what happened in the original post.

Claim by claim

Eight checkable assertions. Six hold or understate, one is a date slip, one fails outright.

Right size
wrong frame
“$103,000,000 … since 2016”

The total is close. Payments through the day of the post came to $102,287,219, within 0.7% of the figure claimed. But it is not money paid since 2016: payments dated 2016‑01‑01 onward total $62,897,039, and roughly 41% of the $103M predates 2016 entirely. Written correctly the claim is stronger — the running total now stands at $105,956,006.

Fails
“On a single day, January 16, 2025, they received $121,728.16”

This does not verify. There is no payment to any of these vendors on 16 January 2025. No row anywhere in the 49-million-row Comptroller dataset carries an amount of 121,728.16, and no vendor-day in the group sums to between 121,727 and 121,730. The nearest candidate is a year later: on 16 January 2026, Beyond Independent Living LLC took 13 payments worth $172,708.26, with a group day total of $190,165.88. Drop the line or replace it with the 2026 figure.

Exact
“A state audit flagging $887,225”

The dollar figure is exact. The Office of the State Auditor published its review on 21 September 2020, covering 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2018. MassHealth paid Beyond Healthcare Agency $1,392,917 for 1,945 GAFC claims covering 117 members, and Finding 1 states the agency was paid as much as $887,225 for group adult foster care that appears to be unallowable, billed on the same day as Home Health Program services. One word needs changing: the post calls these fraudulent claims. The auditor's phrase is “appear to be unallowable” — an upper-bound estimate, not an adjudicated finding. That distinction is the difference between a defensible post and a libel risk.

Under by
two thirds
“At least 12–15 LLCs and corporations tied to her name”

Badly understated. The full pull is 44 distinct entities — 20 filed under Osagiede, 26 under Egah, two appearing in both. They span home health, hospice, adult day health, medical equipment, medical billing, a healthcare school, transportation, software, HVAC, cleaning, real estate, seven churches or ministries, and eight nonprofits.

Confirmed
27 of 44
“Then involuntarily dissolved by the Secretary of the Commonwealth”

Holds. 27 of the 44 entities carry at least one Date of Involuntary Dissolution — 61%. Six of those were later revived and three were dissolved a second time afterwards. Of the seventeen never dissolved, three ceased by conversion or merger rather than by lapse.

Read the dates carefully before writing about them. The Corporations Division runs administrative dissolutions in scheduled batches — late June for corporations, late December for LLCs. Five of these entities share 28 June 2019 and five share 30 June 2025, but that is the state's calendar, not a coordinated event. The pattern worth reporting is the count, not the clustering.
Date differs
“… official notice in February 2025 … before the state revoked its charter”

The nonprofit is real and the revocation is real, but the register dates it differently. Beyond Independent Living Inc (ID 001309461), organised 10 Jan 2018, carries a Date of Involuntary Dissolution of 30 June 2025. February 2025 is entirely consistent with the notice the post reproduces. Write it as “noticed in February, revoked 30 June 2025” and it is airtight.

Exact
“On January 22, 2020, Beyond Independent Living LLC was registered to 661 Centre St”

Confirmed to the day. Entity 001421873, Date of Organization 22 January 2020, records address 661 Centre St, Brockton MA 02302, resident agent and manager Naomi Egah. Two things the post does not say: the address is in Brockton, and this is a second LLC carrying a name identical to the one being paid, which is registered in Boston under the Osagiede spelling. The Fire of Life link also checks out — Fire Of Life. Inc, ID 001150418, is a Chapter 180 religious corporation filed under Naomi Egah at a Brockton address.

Confirmed
“Yet the money keeps flowing”

Understated. Beyond Independent Living LLC has been paid every fiscal year since FY2016, rising from $247,827 to $4,782,426 in FY2026, with $1,125,748 already booked against FY2027. A further $3,608,847 has gone out since the post was published. The most recent payment in the cluster is dated 17 September 2026 — three days before this check was run.

What the post missed

Five findings that do not appear in the original thread. The first two are the substantive ones.

The state paid the audited company while it was legally dissolved

Beyond Healthcare Agency LLC — the entity the State Auditor examined — was involuntarily dissolved by the Secretary of the Commonwealth on 29 December 2023 and not revived until 20 September 2024. Inside that nine-month window the Comptroller records 153 payments totalling $643,388.39, running from 8 January to 16 September 2024.

Payments continue after the surviving corporate entity was dissolved

Since the Beyond Independent Living nonprofit was involuntarily dissolved on 30 June 2025, the Comptroller has paid a vendor named Beyond Independent Living LLC a further 778 payments worth $5,908,174.45, from 7 July 2025 through 17 September 2026 — booked to West Roxbury 02132, the Osagiede address, not the separately registered Brockton LLC of the same name.

Keep this caveat in the copy: a MassHealth provider number and a corporate charter are separate instruments, and a provider can stay enrolled through a filing lapse. The finding is that two of the state's own registers disagree. That is a question for MassHealth, not yet an answer.

A new payee opened as the old one closed

Greater Boston Home Health Care LLC began drawing state money on 20 June 2025 — ten days before the nonprofit's dissolution. It has taken 83 payments worth $47,203 and is still being paid as of 17 September 2026. The payments start at Boston 02132, the Osagiede address, then move to Brockton 02302, the 661 Centre St address, during FY2026. Two LLCs of that name sit in the register, entities 412251357 and 001618231, both under Egah. Small money so far, but it is the newest working vehicle and it is live.

No recoupment is visible anywhere

Across all 7,380 payments there is not a single negative amount; the smallest line in the group is $0.21. No clawback of the $887,225 appears in the state's own checkbook.

Caveat: MassHealth ordinarily nets recoveries against future claims rather than posting a negative to the Comptroller's ledger. Absence here is not proof that nothing was repaid — which is exactly why the public records request is the right next move. Ask for the recoupment determination by name.

Correction: Beyond Adult Day Health Center is not hers

The Comptroller paid a vendor called Beyond Adult Day Health Center, LLC $2,048,436.95 across 616 payments between 2016 and 2020 at a Boston 02132 address. It is a different company. Entity 001175773 was renamed Vilaj Lajwa, LLC on 6 July 2017 and its managers are Victoria Vinokur, Paul Rayev and Gennady Vinokur of Waltham. Do not fold that $2.05M into any total. Beyond Transportation LLC ($124,800, Methuen) and Medical Community Psychotherapy LLC ($10,852, Natick) are excluded on the same grounds — name collisions, no officer link.

Read this before publishing anything

  • None of this establishes fraud. An involuntary dissolution is a failure to file an annual report, not a finding of wrongdoing. The auditor's phrase is “appear to be unallowable”. Every sentence that goes out should survive being read by a lawyer for the other side.
  • The clustered dissolution dates are the state's calendar, not an event. The Corporations Division dissolves delinquent entities in scheduled batches. Report the count, not the coincidence of dates.
  • $105.9M is a floor, not a ceiling. CTHRU records Comptroller disbursements. MassHealth fee-for-service claims appear in it; care paid through a managed care organisation or an ACO generally does not.
  • The appropriation labels are accounting buckets. “Managed Care Plan”, “MassHealth Senior Care” and “Indemnity / Third Party Liability Plan” describe which account the money left, not which service was billed.
  • Thirty-eight of the 44 entities have never received a state payment. That is what the Comptroller's file says, and no more than that — money delivered through a managed care organisation would not appear there.
  • Other people share these surnames. The Osagiede search returns filings by at least a dozen other individuals, including an attorney, a shipping company and several churches. Only the 44 entities listed above carry Naomi Osagiede or Naomi Egah as an officer, manager, agent or signatory.
