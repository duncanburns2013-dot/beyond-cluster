Claim by claim

Eight checkable assertions. Six hold or understate, one is a date slip, one fails outright.

Right size
wrong frame
“$103,000,000 … since 2016”

The total is close. Payments through the day of the post came to $102,287,219, within 0.7% of the figure claimed. But it is not money paid since 2016: payments dated 2016‑01‑01 onward total $62,897,039, and roughly 41% of the $103M predates 2016 entirely. Written correctly the claim is stronger — the running total now stands at $105,956,006.

Fails
“On a single day, January 16, 2025, they received $121,728.16”

This does not verify. There is no payment to any of these vendors on 16 January 2025. No row anywhere in the 49-million-row Comptroller dataset carries an amount of 121,728.16, and no vendor-day in the group sums to between 121,727 and 121,730. The nearest candidate is a year later: on 16 January 2026, Beyond Independent Living LLC took 13 payments worth $172,708.26, with a group day total of $190,165.88. Drop the line or replace it with the 2026 figure.

Exact
“A state audit flagging $887,225”

The dollar figure is exact. The Office of the State Auditor published its review on 21 September 2020, covering 1 January 2016 to 31 December 2018. MassHealth paid Beyond Healthcare Agency $1,392,917 for 1,945 GAFC claims covering 117 members, and Finding 1 states the agency was paid as much as $887,225 for group adult foster care that appears to be unallowable, billed on the same day as Home Health Program services. One word needs changing: the post calls these fraudulent claims. The auditor's phrase is “appear to be unallowable” — an upper-bound estimate, not an adjudicated finding. That distinction is the difference between a defensible post and a libel risk.

Under by
two thirds
“At least 12–15 LLCs and corporations tied to her name”

Badly understated. The full pull is 45 distinct entities — 20 filed under Osagiede, 26 under Egah, one appearing in both. They span home health, hospice, adult day health, medical equipment, medical billing, a healthcare school, transportation, software, HVAC, cleaning, real estate, seven churches or ministries, and eight nonprofits.

Confirmed
27 of 45
“Then involuntarily dissolved by the Secretary of the Commonwealth”

Holds. 27 of the 45 entities carry at least one Date of Involuntary Dissolution — 60%. Six of those were later revived and three were dissolved a second time afterwards. Of the eighteen never dissolved, three ceased by conversion or merger rather than by lapse.

Read the dates carefully before writing about them. Five of these entities share 28 June 2019 and five share 30 June 2025, and the LLC dissolutions fall on 29–31 December. Neither statute prescribes a date: MGL c.156D §14.21 for corporations and MGL c.156C §70 for LLCs each require written notice and a 90-day cure period, after which the Secretary shall dissolve. The clustering is an observed pattern in the filing data, consistent with batch processing; I have not found a published schedule and do not assert one. The figure worth reporting is the count, not the coincidence of dates.
Date differs
“… official notice in February 2025 … before the state revoked its charter”

The nonprofit is real and the revocation is real, but the register dates it differently. Beyond Independent Living Inc (ID 001309461), organised 10 Jan 2018, carries a Date of Involuntary Dissolution of 30 June 2025. February 2025 is entirely consistent with the notice the post reproduces. Write it as “noticed in February, revoked 30 June 2025” and it is airtight.

Confirmed to the day. Entity 001421873, Date of Organization 22 January 2020, records address 661 Centre St, Brockton MA 02302, resident agent and manager Naomi Egah. What is actually provable

Worth being exact about, because the gap between what the records show and what a reader will assume is where this goes wrong.

Provable from documents today, no inference

One person operates 45 Massachusetts entities under two filing names, tied together by three independent records, one of them federal. 27 carry an involuntary dissolution. All seven that held federal tax exemption lost it for failure to file. The entity revoked in May 2021 has been paid $32.4M, including $4.78M in FY2026. Beyond Healthcare Agency was paid $643,388 across 153 payments while dissolved. A 2020 audit put up to $887,225 in apparently unallowable billing with no recovery in any public file. Every company still holds an active federal billing number.

Answerable with one more record, which nobody has yet

Whether MassHealth ever recouped the $887,225, and whether provider enrolment lapsed during the dissolution. Both sit in MassHealth’s provider file. The public records request is the way to get them.

Not provable by anyone outside government

Whether services were delivered, whether any billing was knowingly false, and intent. Those need claims-level data and a subpoena. The defensible claim is not that someone stole $103 million. It is that a single operator with 45 companies, 27 administrative dissolutions, seven revoked exemptions and an unresolved audit finding has been paid $105.9 million and is still being paid, and that no public body appears to have looked. That is a demand for an audit, and the records carry it.

What the post missed

Five findings that do not appear in the original thread. The first two are the substantive ones.

The state paid the audited company while it was legally dissolved

Beyond Healthcare Agency LLC — the entity the State Auditor examined — was involuntarily dissolved by the Secretary of the Commonwealth on 29 December 2023 and not revived until 20 September 2024. Inside that nine-month window the Comptroller records 153 payments totalling $643,388.39, running from 8 January to 16 September 2024.

Payments continue after the surviving corporate entity was dissolved

Since the Beyond Independent Living nonprofit was involuntarily dissolved on 30 June 2025, the Comptroller has paid a vendor named Beyond Independent Living LLC a further 778 payments worth $5,908,174.45, from 7 July 2025 through 17 September 2026 — booked to West Roxbury 02132, the Osagiede address, not the separately registered Brockton LLC of the same name.

Read this before calling it a violation, because it is close to the opposite of one. A MassHealth provider number and a corporate charter are separate instruments. 130 CMR 450.212 sets out ten provider eligibility criteria and none of them requires active corporate registration, good standing, or notice to MassHealth of a dissolution. 130 CMR 450.231 conditions payment on the provider being a participating provider on the date of service and says nothing about corporate status. On the face of those two sections, paying a dissolved company breaks no rule — and that is the finding. Two of the Commonwealth's registers disagree and nothing in the billing regulation requires them to agree. A legislator can close that gap; an auditor cannot.

A new payee opened as the old one closed

Greater Boston Home Health Care LLC began drawing state money on 20 June 2025 — ten days before the nonprofit's dissolution. It has taken 83 payments worth $47,203 and is still being paid as of 17 September 2026. The payments start at Boston 02132, the Osagiede address, then move to Brockton 02302, the 661 Centre St address, during FY2026. Two LLCs of that name sit in the register, entities 412251357 and 001618231, both under Egah — and because CTHRU carries no entity ID, the payments cannot be attributed between them. Small money so far, but it is the newest working vehicle and it is live.

No recoupment is visible anywhere

Across all 7,380 payments there is not a single negative amount; the smallest line in the group is $0.21. No clawback of the $887,225 appears in the state's own checkbook.

Absence is not proof that nothing was repaid, but the reason is narrower than it looks: CTHRU records money paid out by the Commonwealth, and money returned to it is revenue that would sit in a different file. What the regulations say is worth quoting. 130 CMR 450.235 requires a provider to report in writing and return an identified overpayment within 60 days. 130 CMR 450.237 gives MassHealth a notice-and-determination process and says the agency will take appropriate action to recover. Neither section, as written, describes netting an overpayment against future claims. Ask for the overpayment determination by name.

Correction: Beyond Adult Day Health Center is not hers

The Comptroller paid a vendor called Beyond Adult Day Health Center, LLC $2,048,436.95 across 616 payments between 2016 and 2020 at a Boston 02132 address. It is a different company. Entity 001175773 was renamed Vilaj Lajwa, LLC on 6 July 2017 and its managers are Victoria Vinokur, Paul Rayev and Gennady Vinokur of Waltham. Do not fold that $2.05M into any total. Beyond Transportation LLC ($124,800, Methuen) and Medical Community Psychotherapy LLC ($10,852, Natick) are excluded on the same grounds — name collisions, no officer link.

Read this before publishing anything

  • None of this establishes fraud. An involuntary dissolution is a failure to file an annual report, not a finding of wrongdoing. The auditor's phrase is “appear to be unallowable”. Every sentence that goes out should survive being read by a lawyer for the other side.
  • Do not read the clustered dissolution dates as a single event. MGL c.156D §14.21 and c.156C §70 each prescribe written notice and a 90-day cure period, and neither names a date. The clustering is an observed pattern in the filing data, not a published schedule. Report the count, not the coincidence of dates.
  • $105.9M is a floor, not a ceiling. CTHRU records disbursements made by the Comptroller. I have not established which payment routes do and do not land in that file, so treat the total as everything the Comptroller's own ledger shows, not as everything these companies were paid.
  • The appropriation labels are accounting buckets. “Managed Care Plan”, “MassHealth Senior Care” and “Indemnity / Third Party Liability Plan” describe which account the money left, not which service was billed.
  • Forty-one of the 45 entities show no state payment at all. That is what the Comptroller's file says, and no more than that — money reaching a provider by a route other than a Comptroller disbursement would not appear there.
  • Other people share these surnames. The Osagiede search returns filings by at least a dozen other individuals, including an attorney, a shipping company and several churches. Only the 45 entities listed above carry Naomi Osagiede or Naomi Egah as an officer, manager, agent or signatory.

  • Retrieved. All figures 20 September 2026; payment data current to 17 September 2026. The audit script re-derives every one of them from the live sources and fails on any disagreement.
  • Read in full. All 45 entity summaries at the Corporations Division, pulled individually. The State Auditor report of 21 September 2020. Every row these EINs return from the IRS Automatic Revocation list. Every NPPES record for the seven billing numbers.
